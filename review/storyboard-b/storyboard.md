# Storyboard b — Where the Water Goes

> The `work/` and `site/` folders named here (the scripts and the site copy that made these images) stay in the working folder and are not part of this review set.

**Logline.** A small hovering creature digs one hollow in bare ground, and the film follows the rain it gathers down
through roots and strata to water underground, where a handmade wheel, turned by that same water, lifts it back up to
a garden. Over a sped-up season the whole column, above ground and below, fills with life, and the camera rises up
the well and pulls back onto the cross-section that keeps running under the film.

Draft b of three. Its lens is depth: the vertical carries the story. The camera goes down with the water in Acts II
and III and comes back up in Act IV, retracing the water's path in reverse (wheel, shaft, well, garden) before
the pull-back. Every act and question in the brief is covered below.

- 60.000 s, 72 beats at 72 bpm (a beat is 5/6 s; beat *n* is at *n* × 5/6 s). 38 rows.
- Title bar throughout: `silt` / `concept render · time sped up`.
- The creature in every picture is a **placeholder creature**: a soft rounded body in #E2C29C with a #C9A47E
  underside, a #FBEBD2 highlight and two #221917 eyes. It hovers and has no feet. The real design is being chosen
  separately.
- World coordinates below are in Exhibit B's grains at 1440 × 900, dpr 1: 576 × 324 grains, ground line at row 105.
  "Cells a grain" is how many picture cells (2 CSS px each at 1440) one of those grains covers.

Key frames: `kf1-10.000.png`, `kf2-21.667.png`, `kf3-36.667.png`, `kf4-43.333.png`, `kf5-54.167.png`.
The ending: `end-desktop.png`, `end-phone.png`. The zoom-out: `zoom.png`.

## The shape of it

| Act | Time | Where the camera is | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. A small beginning | 0.000–13.333 | the bare surface, in close-up | `silt` at a prompt; the creature plants a seed, sets a stake and digs a hollow. |
| II. The world responds | 13.333–28.333 | the 1:1 view, tilting down | Rain fills the hollow into a pond. Grass and roots follow the damp, and birds come to drink. The moisture lens shows the water sinking and gathering underground. |
| III. Discovery through engineering | 28.333–45.000 | close-up, going **down** a shaft; then the 1:1 view of the whole column | The creature digs down to the water underground and builds a wheel and a chain of pots. When the pool rises to its lip, the spill turns the wheel, the pots lift water up the shaft to a bed it sowed, and the rest of the spill wakes the fungus cave. |
| IV. A living universe | 45.000–60.000 | 1:1 time-lapse; close-up at the wheel, rising **up** the well; pull-back | Days run. The column fills, above and below, and dusk comes. The camera follows a pot up the well to the creature at its cabbages, then pulls back onto Exhibit B's framing. |

The causal chain the viewer can follow: the hollow (I) → the pond → the seep → the pool → the spill → the wheel
→ the pots → the bed → water soaking back down to the pool (III). The same spill also runs down to the cave, where the
caps grow (III, IV). It is one loop, and it is the one Exhibit B describes: "The water goes round and is never
made or lost."

### What is simulated, and what is choreographed but caused

| Event | How it is caused |
|---|---|
| Rain darkening the bare silt; water running into the hollow and filling it | Simulated (water grains, soak), as today's water. |
| The sapling sprouting; grass in a ring round the pond; reeds at the water | Simulated: damp plus time, and growth only on damp grains. The ring follows the damp. |
| Roots growing down after the damp; worms moving into it | Simulated: root tips and worms step toward the wetter neighbour. |
| Birds arriving at the pond | Choreographed paths, but released only when the pond's standing water passes a count taken from the arrays. |
| The seep down to the stone and along it to the hollow | Choreographed but driven. A cheap moisture field (diffusion through the ground, blocked by stone) moves in film time; the lens draws that field; drips into the chamber start where it reaches the chamber's roof, at a rate that follows it. |
| The pool rising to its lip on beat 50.5 | The drips are real water grains. The script guarantees a minimum rate so every seed reaches the lip on time, and story.cjs gates it. |
| The wheel turning | Choreographed rotation whose speed is set by the water grains crossing the lip each frame (counted), so it starts when the spill starts and slows if the spill does. |
| Pots, trough and the bed sprouting | The pots carry water grains up and pour them into the trough; the bed sprouts from that damp (simulated threshold). |
| Caps in the cave | They grow only on cave sand made damp by the spill (simulated threshold), as fungus does in Exhibit B. |
| Act IV structures and trees | Dated growth from Exhibit B's composition, revealed day by day, as today's dated valley. Each part appears next to its cause: trees and flowers on damp ground, terraces stepping out from the trough, the cabin by the well. The creature is shown at each job. |

### 6. The camera language

Grains are always whole device pixels: at 1440, 1 cell is 2 CSS px (2 device px at dpr 1, 4 at dpr 2); on a
phone, 1 CSS px (3 device px). Nothing is ever scaled by a fraction.

- **Pans.** Across (Act I), down (the Act II tilt and the Act III descent) and up (the Act III return and the Act IV
  rise). Pans move in whole cells. Slow ones keep a steady cadence (one cell every 3 frames in the tilt). Fast ones are
  eased integer steps of at most 3 cells a frame: the descent and the rise run at about 80 cells a second, about 160
  CSS px a second at 1440. Every grain sits on the pixel grid in every frame, so nothing shimmers.
- **Gentle zooms.**
  - At beats 16, 34, 48 and 58, a **dissolve between two whole-cell pictures**: 12 frames of crossfade on the beat,
    centred on the creature or the wheel.
  - The final pull-back **steps through four native levels** (2.4, 1.6, 1.2, 1.0 cells a grain). Each level is drawn
    at its own grain size, with a 6-frame crossfade on the beat, so it reads as a gentle zoom made of whole-cell
    pictures.
- **Time-lapses.** Hard day steps on the half-beats with the camera still and the light held at afternoon. The light
  changes only slowly (golden by beat 58, dusk by the rise), so nothing flickers.
- **Light through the film.**

  | Part | Light |
  |---|---|
  | Act I | Dawn to morning |
  | Act II | A shower, then sun |
  | Act III, underground | Daylight down the shaft and the creature's soft glow |
  | Act IV | Afternoon through the time-lapse, then golden, then dusk, matching Exhibit B |

- **Reduced motion.**
  - Dissolves become hard cuts on the same beats, and the tilt becomes one cut at beat 26.
  - The descent and the rise become two hard cuts each (beats 35, 37, 39 and 59, 61, 63).
  - The pull-back becomes three hard steps (64, 65, 66), and the rings show as still outlines for one beat each.
  - Days pass in four steps (6, 21, 57, 120).
  - Nothing twinkles: fireflies, spores, water shimmer and the glow of the caps hold still; the lens dims without its
    dither crawling.
  - The wheel turns in four fixed poses a beat.
- **Narrower desktops, 947–1279 px.** The picture follows the action as today. Shots are set by landmarks (the pond
  at x 180, the well at x 386, the wheel at x 346), so the creature and the subject of each beat stay in frame.

### 7. The counters and panes

- **`day N` stays** on the left of the ticker as the gentle sense of time. It steps only when time is sped up (1–3 in
  Act II, 3–6 in Act III, 6–120 in Act IV) and goes at the end.
- **The right of the ticker:** `N grains growing` goes. In its place is a quiet location line: `surface` at the
  ground, `depth N` below it. N is the number of rows between the creature and the ground surface above it in its
  column, counted from the material array, in Exhibit B's grains. The line is empty during the time-lapse and the end.

- **The panes:** `new material: clay` and `new life: reeds` go. The pane row shows only the lens in use, and never a

  - `moisture  dry ▪▪▪ wet` in Act II: per-grain water, from the moisture array.

  Discoveries are shown by the creature's own reaction (eyes widen, a small bounce) instead of a pane.
- **Keys row:** `[p] plant   [d] dig   [b] build   [i] inspect   [z] zoom`. There is one new key, `[i] inspect`, lit

## Risks, and what would be hard to build

- **The addition needs a yes.** It also has to be drawn and animated in Exhibit B without touching B's dice: the
  wheel, the recess and the pots go in as an overlay, and the wheel's speed is read from B's own spill at the lip. If
  the answer is no, the twin-shaft fallback works but is weaker.
- **The creature is bigger than Exhibit B's underground spaces.** The shaft is 4 grains inside, the pool's air pocket
  6–7 rows and the steps about 8 rows, against a creature 10 grains across. The beat list keeps it out of those spaces.
  story.cjs should gate that the creature never overlaps solid grains in any view.
- **Building Exhibit B at the film's grain sizes costs time.** Measured on this (busy, 4-CPU) machine, composing plus
  the 180 settling updates plus rendering:

  | Grain size | Time |
  |---|---|
  | U 1.5 | 0.36 s |
  | U 1.8 | 0.64 s |
  | U 2.4 | 0.92 s |
  | U 3.6 | 2.36 s |

  These must be built in slices before they are needed (U 1.8 by beat 54, U 3.6 by 58, U 2.4 by 63). Memory for the
  U 3.6 world (1383 × 778 grains, several arrays) matters on phones.
- **The seep is far faster than a plain soak rule would carry water 100 grains down.** It is choreographed but
  driven, as described above. The pool must reach its lip on beat 50.5 for every seed, so it needs a guaranteed
  minimum rate and a story.cjs gate.
- **The handover from simulated to composed grains at beat 54** could pop, especially the pond's shape and the grass.
  It is hidden in the first day step, which widens the pond anyway, but needs a careful look.
- **Narrow desktops.** At 1100 and 1000, Exhibit B's underground is squeezed, and a band of 217 rows barely holds the
  chimney and the cave floor. The rule may need a per-width check, or a two-row trim of the smoke.
- **The visible settle.** Exhibit B's settle in the last 4.5 s could show water levelling. The fallback is to hold
  the settled frame.
- **Speed of the vertical moves.** The descent and the rise are the fastest moves, at about 160 CSS px a second.
  Keep them eased; if they feel hurried, stretch the rise to 5 beats and take the beat from the time-lapse.
- **The phone ending is a crop of the 1440 layout, not Exhibit B's phone layout.** It reads as the same place but is
  not the same frame.
- **The knock on every beat from 42.5 s** could feel mechanical. Keep it soft, well under the pad, and vary its timbre
  slightly; the wheel's creak is every 4 beats. The new cues (rain, key ticks, knock, creak, clay, echoing drips) all
  have to come from the existing generator's oscillators, noise and filters.
- **Busy scenes.** The 12-cell creature at 1:1 must stay readable among the Act IV parts. Its colours were checked
  in kf2, kf4 and kf5; the final design should be checked the same way.

## How the mockups were made

These are mockups, not renders.

- **The base.** Exhibit B's opening, built at the film's grain sizes by a copy of the site in `site/`. Its `sand.js`
  gains a hook, `__plateAt(U, …)`, that composes, settles 180 updates and renders at any U with today's creature
  removed after the settle, so the dice are unchanged. At U 1.5 the hook reproduces `ref/cross-section.png` pixel for
  pixel.
- **Mid-film frames.** Exhibit B's later parts are painted out (pond basin, cellar, shaft and roots filled from the
  ground beside them; the surface repainted bare; voids dried), and the sky is repainted for the time of day.
- **New elements are drawn by hand at the right cell scale**: the hollow, grass, roots, birds, wheel, belt, pots,
  trough, caps, lenses, rings and the placeholder creature.
- **Display.** Every picture is 577 × 257 cells shown at exactly 2 CSS px a cell, enlarged with nearest-neighbour only.
  The text uses `kit/plex-mono.ttf` on the 9 × 27 grid.
- **Labels.** Annotations are pale blue tags, which are not part of the film.
