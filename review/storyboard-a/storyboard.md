# Storyboard A: One Window

> The `work/` and `site/` folders named here (the scripts and the site copy that made these images) stay in the working folder and are not part of this review set.

**Logline.** One patient camera watches a single bare place, laid out in Exhibit B's own geography, become Exhibit B: a seed, a dug hollow and a plank let the rain in, the water finds its way down to a dark cave, and a small wheel set in that water goes on watering the garden by itself. The film's first wide view and its last working view are the same framing, first bare and then full, before a gentle pull-back lands on the cross-section that carries on live below the film.

Lens for this draft: continuity first. There are no hard cuts after the launch. The camera makes two crossfades between whole-cell scales, two slow tilts and one final pull-back. The viewer can read Exhibit B's geography from the first wide moment (15.8 s, `kf2`) and watches that same place fill in (`kf4`).

**Cause and effect.**
- **Simulated:** the dig, the rain running into the hollow, the damp spreading out and down, grass and reeds following the damp, the stair filling step by step, and the water reaching the cave.

### 6. The camera language

- **One camera, no hard cuts after the launch.** It makes two crossfades and two slow tilts, then the final pull-back, which is the third crossfade. Each crossfade blends two pictures that are each drawn in whole cells, mixed cell by cell over 1.5–2 beats. No grain is ever resampled, so nothing shimmers. The first crossfade is an exact 2:1: each 1:1 cell is 2 × 2 close-up cells.
- **Tilts move in whole cells**: 58 cells over 2.5 s, about 23 cells (46 CSS px) a second, which is one 2-px step every two or three frames at 60 fps.
- **Time-lapses happen in place.** The camera holds or tilts while the days step and the light changes.
- **No resampled zoom.** A smooth zoom between 2.4 and 1.2 cells a grain has no whole-cell scale in between, so it is never attempted.
- **Phones:** the same moves, cropped to the action. The final pull-back goes one level further, to 0.5.
- **Reduced motion:**
  - the crossfades become hard cuts on their first beat (14.167, 52.500);
  - each tilt becomes one hard step (at 23.333 and 47.083, the middle of each tilt);
  - the days pass in four hard steps (6 → 21 → 61 → 120, at beats 52, 55, 58, 60);
  - nothing twinkles: the cursor doesn't blink, the fireflies and fungi glow steadily, and the water doesn't shimmer;
  - the overlays and rings appear and go without fades;
  - the wheel turns in quarter-turn steps on the beat, so its cause and effect still read.

### 7. The counters and panes

- **Drop "N grains growing"** and the "new material / new life" panes. The creature's restrained discovery reaction (eyes widen, a small bounce or a brief brightening) stands in for them at beats 22, 40 and 52.
- **Keep "day N"** on the ticker's left. It is the film's own gentle clock (steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, then 6 → 120 in the time-lapse), and it clears when the final frame lands. The ticker's right side stays empty.
- **Two occasional inspection overlays**, named in the pane row while they are on:
  - `inspect · moisture` (20.0–22.9 s): each grain tinted by its own water, read from the arrays;
  - `inspect · flow` (40.8–42.9 s): it traces the grains that moved this second, the same stamp Exhibit B uses for its flowing-water colour.

  Neither shows a number. If the owner ever wants one, it must be counted from the arrays, such as "damp ground: N grains"; this draft shows none.
- **One new key: `[i] inspect`.** The keys row becomes `[p] plant  [d] dig  [b] build  [i] inspect  [z] zoom`, with the key in use lit. On phones, only the lit key shows, as today.

## The launch

`silt` is typed at a bare `$` prompt in the picture area for about two seconds (0.0–2.1 s). There is no install command, package manager, URL or version. The world then fades up inside the same window.

## What stays undecided

The film shows one place and one creature. It does not say whether the world is huge or a room that grows: B's edges simply carry on. It shows no goal, and it doesn't say whether the creature is picked from a set or customised. Nobody else appears, and the tagline and name appear only as working words. The title bar keeps "concept render · time sped up", and the end keeps "Concept render · in development · working title".

## Exhibit B changes this storyboard asks the owner to approve

The new creature, which is already decided, plus **one proposal**: the small wheel, pot loop and trough described in question 4. The proposal is about 70 grains in the frozen frame and moves only when the stair runs, and it needs the owner's yes. Without it, the fallback leaves Exhibit B unchanged apart from the creature.

## Risks, and what would be hard to build

- **Sharing B's composition.** `boot()` lives in a DOM-bound generator inside `sand.js` (about 2,600 lines). It has to become a DOM-free module that `reel-core.js` and `story.cjs` can run under Node, and B's frozen frame has to stay byte-identical (checked with `plate.mjs`).
- **Building B's plan at the film's grains.** U = 3.6 gives 1382 × 778 grains, a million cells, for one close-up. The film needs a windowed build (C plus a margin) to keep load time and memory down on phones. U = 1.8 is the same grain size B already builds at dpr 3.
- **Two sets of rules.** The film's working ground (water, soak, damp, grass) runs on `reel-core`'s rules over B's materials. The dated hand-over in Act IV hides any mismatch, but deciding which day each of B's grains appears on is a new authoring job. Most of it can follow from the geometry: plants by their distance from water, fungi from the cave pool, structures in build order.
- **The crossfades.** For 1.5–2 beats two pictures are painted at once, doubling the paint cost. During a crossfade the two scales show at once; if that reads as a double image, shorten it to one beat.
- **The 283-row end picture** changes `reel.js`: the canvas has to grow over the freed strip at the end. The cover still, poster frame and mp4 need re-rendering to match.
- **Phones need a 2:1 reduction of B** for their last frame, with lights kept. That is a small new paint path.
- **Act III's legibility.** At 1:1 the wheel is only about 26 CSS px across, and the chain has four parts (shaft, wheel, pots, trough). The flow overlay is there to carry it. If testing says it is still too small, add a one-beat push-in to the close-up level at beats 42–46. That costs two more crossfades.
- **The final hold** (54.2–60.0 s) must end exactly on the frozen frame. Only motion that returns to its starting state is allowed: fireflies and fungi pulse their glow in place and the creature bobs; nothing drifts.
- **The creature needs a 12-cell version** at 1:1, in addition to the 24 × 24 and 16 × 16 versions in the creature plan. The 10 × 9 version in B should be its pixel-for-pixel reduction.
- **The soundtrack.** The wheel's half-beat knock must stay soft or it turns busy; it fades out by beat 58. The three new cues are synthesisable from damped sines and filtered noise: a wooden knock, a creak, and a pour.
- **Exhibit B's live scene.** If the addition is approved, B gains a new behaviour (the wheel turns while the stair runs) that has to keep "never made or lost" true.
