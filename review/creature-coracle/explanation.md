# Coracle: why this one

Coracle is a small bowl of fired clay that rides the air the way a round boat rides water, with one lit bead hanging
beneath it on a short line, tied a little to one side so the bead never hangs plumb under the middle. You know it at any
size, even in solid black: a level lip over a round hull and a drop of light below, just off centre. Nothing else in the
world has that outline, and it needs no limbs, ears or face beyond two quiet eyes. Everything it does goes through the
bead: it lets it down to press in a seed, tap a grain home or give a wheel its first turn, so it touches the world one
grain at a time and never looks powerful. The clay is fired and hardly gives; its life is in a lean, a look, and the way
the bead lags and catches up, which is calm enough to watch for hours. It is the same size in the world in every view,
from the opening close-up to the last frame of Exhibit B. It is made of the same earth it tends, which fits a place
where every afternoon leaves a layer, and at night, when the clay dims with everything else, its eyes and its bead stay
lit like a lantern let down on a string.
