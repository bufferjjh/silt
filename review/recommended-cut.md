# The recommended cut: storyboard A, with the reviewers' grafts

Storyboard A ("One Window") keeps the spine that makes the owner's decision literal. One place, laid out in Exhibit B's own geography, is seen bare at the first wide moment (A's kf2). The same framing is seen full at the end of Act IV (A's kf4). Then one gentle pull-back lands on Exhibit B. What follows is A with the fixes the three reviewers agreed on. Times are on the 72 bpm grid (a beat is 5/6 s).

## The section 5 answers for this cut

- **Ending, desktop:** Exhibit B rows 0–283 of 324 (283½ rows show), across its full 576-grain width. The moon, the tree tops, the cabin, the pond, the terraces, the well, the water underground and the fungus cave are all in. Only the bottom rock and the spiral fossil are left out. It is drawn by Exhibit B's own code, so it is the frozen frame exactly. The film box keeps its 25 rows. See `ending/end-1440-285rows.png`.
- **Ending, phone:** option (i) in `ending/end-phone-options.png`. It is a band of the same composition the phone has been watching, at 1 CSS px a grain, framed on the cabin, the well and the water below. That keeps the last move a true pull-back. Exhibit B's own phone picture uses a different layout (no terraces), so landing on it would mean a jump, not a pull-back, unless the phone film were laid out on that layout from the start (as storyboard C does).
- **Every seed:** from Act IV every grain is dated, and the last frame is drawn by Exhibit B's code with its fixed dice. A new check makes every seed from 1 to 256 end on seed 10's frame, pixel for pixel. See `ending/approach.md`.
- **Tree rings:** kept. The film's two real earlier views ride the pull-back and are gone 2.5 beats before the tagline.
- **The waterwheel and machinery:** the owner's choice, below.
- **Zoom-out:** 1.33× more world across in both working views. The close-up shows the 24×24 sprite and the 1:1 a 12-cell version, so the close-up stays 2× the 1:1 as today. The final pull-back is a further 1.2×. A milder 1.2× is possible (the 1:1 at Exhibit B's own dpr-2 grain, a 13-cell creature). See `storyboard-a/zoom.png`.
- **Camera:** one camera and no hard cuts after the launch. There are per-cell dithered dissolves between whole-cell scales, steady whole-cell tilts, time-lapses in place, and one push-in for the wheel. Reduced motion turns these into hard steps on the same beats.
- **Counters and panes:** "N grains growing" and the material and life panes go. "day N" stays as the film's clock. Two short inspection views (moisture, flow) are named in the pane row and show no numbers. One new key: `[i] inspect`.

## The one choice that needs the owner's yes: where the wheel goes

1. **Inside Exhibit B (recommended, if the owner agrees).** A small wooden wheel at the top of the underground stair, the well's rope as a loop of clay pots, and a short trough to the cabbage bed: about 70 grains in the frozen frame. It is drawn on top at render time with no dice, so nothing else in Exhibit B changes, and it turns in the live scene only while water runs down the stair. The film's whole story then happens in the place the last frame shows, and the machine the creature built is still there, running, in the scene below the film.
2. **Outside Exhibit B (if not).** Storyboard C's geography: just past Exhibit B's east edge, a spring runs away from the garden. The creature builds the wheel there to lift water back over the crest by a trough to the terraces. Act III pans east at 1:1 and back. Exhibit B stays exactly as it is apart from the creature. The wheel is just out of the last frame, and one far knock is heard under it.
