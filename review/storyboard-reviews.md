# Storyboard reviews

Three independent reviewers read all three storyboards in full and looked at every mockup. They checked each beat list against the 72 bpm grid (all three total 60.000 s) and checked that every section 5 question is answered (all three answer all seven). Each reviewer took one lens:

- **Owner's voice:** the owner's own answers and Appendix A first. Calm and awe, the additive "how long have you been working at this" feeling, cause and effect you can follow, very few words.
- **Film and sound:** pacing on the grid, letting moments linger, camera language, the sound cues, and whether the landing on Exhibit B satisfies.
- **Build:** whole-pixel grains, every seed the same, the last frame equal to Exhibit B's frozen frame, phones and narrow windows, speed, and Exhibit B left alone.

## What each reviewer said, in short

**Owner's voice (C, A, B).**
- C's hard choices are the decided ones: Exhibit B stays exactly as it is, the machine sits outside it, the last frame shows B's rows 0–283 with the tagline over the picture, and the phone lands on B's own phone picture.
- C's faults are editing faults:
  - Act IV is crowded;
  - the camera goes out, in and out again before the end;
  - the rings appear twice.
- A is the calmest and has the purest additive moment, but its wheel is too small to read and its phone ending is a half-size thumbnail.
- B has the best cause and effect, but it is the least calm.

**Film and sound (A, C, B).**
- A is the calmest, most lingering cut. It is the only one whose first wide view and last working view are the same framing, bare and then full. Its rings are the film's real earlier views, and its 1.2× pull-back is the gentlest landing.
- C has the best end frame, the best sound design and the clearest engineering logic. But its action happens outside Exhibit B for 39 s, its wheel lifts water rather than driving machinery, and its ending goes out and in again.
- B has the best causal map and the best descent, but it crushes the living-universe act into 3.3 s.

**Build (A, C, B).**
- A turns the owner's decision into one continuous film, needs the lightest builds, and follows the ending study's path most closely.
- C is the only one that leaves Exhibit B untouched. But its tended patch sits outside B for 40 s, and its world and script would change with the stage width.
- B needs the heaviest builds and moves the camera fast.

## What all three would graft onto the winner

**From B:**
- the damp running sideways along the hard stone to the water underground, shown in the moisture view (this fixes a gap in A, where the hollow is about 150 grains from the water underground);
- a short close-up for the wheel's build and first turn;
- the wheel built dry and started by the rising water;
- the creature never squeezed into a space smaller than itself, with a check for it.

**From C:**
- the tagline over the picture, on plain rock at the foot of the frame;
- the time-lapse at the same hour each day, so nothing flickers;
- clay as a new-material discovery;
- the machine's knock playing the pulse softly and fading;
- the phone ending landing on a real band of Exhibit B rather than a half-size reduction;
- the off-frame crest east of Exhibit B as the ready-made place for the wheel if the owner says no to adding it.

**For A itself:**
- the pull-back as a per-cell dithered dissolve of about a beat, not an alpha blend;
- a steady whole-cell cadence for the tilts.
